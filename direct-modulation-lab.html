Direct Modulation Lab

Switch the laser current and the light follows, but not at once: electrons and photons trade energy back and forth at the relaxation frequency, f_R ∝ √(I − I_th), and the carrier swing also shifts the optical frequency (chirp). Drive the laser with a step or with bits and find where direct modulation runs out.

Rate equations of slides 67 and 72, integrated with Runge–Kutta (0.25 ps steps), same InGaAsP laser as the L–I Curve Lab (L = 250 µm, R = 0.31, α_int = 10 cm⁻¹, τ_p ≈ 2 ps, τ_e = 2.2 ns, I_th ≈ 12.8 mA), plus gain compression G = G_N(N − N₀)/(1 + εP) with ε = 10⁻⁷ per photon, which damps the oscillations as on a real chip. The response |H(f)| is the exact small-signal solution of the linearized equations around the bias (slides 73–74). The frequency shift Δν = (α/4π)[G − 1/τ_p] contains the transient chirp (where the power changes) and the adiabatic chirp (from gain compression). The current has a 15 ps electrical rise time. The bit pattern is a PRBS-7 sequence; the eye overlays all of it.
