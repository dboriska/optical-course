Einstein Processes Lab

Light meets two-level atoms in three ways. Absorption lifts an electron from E₁ to E₂ and removes a photon. Spontaneous emission drops it back at a random time and sends a photon in a random direction. Stimulated emission is triggered by a passing photon and adds an exact copy of it. Send a beam through the atoms, then pump them and watch the beam start to grow.

A toy model: 70 two-level atoms, B₁₂ = B₂₁ (equal degeneracy), so a beam photon that crosses an atom interacts with the same probability (12%) whether the atom is up or down: absorbed if it is down, copied if it is up. Up atoms decay spontaneously with lifetime τ; the pump lifts down atoms at rate W. Rates are counted over the last 3 seconds of simulation time. Right panel, thermal equilibrium only (slides 32–33): Rst/Rsp = 1/(ehν/kT − 1) and N₂/N₁ = e−hν/kT. At 1550 nm and 300 K the ratio is about 4·10⁻¹⁴: without pumping, spontaneous emission wins by far. Thermal excitation is left out of the simulation for the same reason.
