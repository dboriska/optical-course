Optical Communication Labs

Interactive demonstrations for the course lectures. They run in any browser, on a laptop or a phone.

Lecture 02 · Optical Fibers

Snell's Law Lab Change the angle and the refractive indices and watch rays and waves refract, reach the critical angle and turn into total internal reflection. Includes Fresnel reflectance. Acceptance Cone and NA Lab Aim a ray at the fiber face and change n₁, Δ and the core diameter. See when the ray is guided or escapes, and the acceptance cone in 3D. Graded-Index Ray Race Rays race through a step-index and a graded-index fiber, with the core colored by the speed of light. See why the parabolic profile keeps the pulse together, and where each ray turns back (n(x) = β).

Lecture 03 · Modes of Optical Fibers

V Number and Mode Chart Lab Change the wavelength, the core radius and the index contrast. See the V number, the cutoff wavelength and which modes are guided on the b–V chart, with a picture of each mode. Spot Size Lab Compare the exact fundamental mode with its Gaussian approximation. See the spot size, the mode field diameter, the effective area and the power in the core change with V.

Lecture 04 · Chromatic Dispersion

Crest vs Envelope One wave packet, two speeds: the crests move at the phase velocity and the envelope at the group velocity. Phase vs Group Velocity Add two waves of slightly different frequency and watch the beats. See vp and vg on the ω–k diagram and why they differ when there is dispersion. Phase and Group Velocity Lab The carrier moves at c/n, the envelope that carries the data at c/ng. Change the wavelength and see the group index and the delay of a fiber of length L. Pulse Spreading Lab Each frequency in the pulse travels at its own group velocity, so the pulse spreads by ΔT = |D|·L·Δλ. Launch a pulse and watch neighbouring bits start to overlap. Rate and Reach Map How far can each fiber carry a given bit rate? Compare the modal-dispersion limit of multimode fiber with the chromatic-dispersion limit of single-mode fiber, and drag the dot to place your link.

Lecture 05 · Pulse Dispersion and Chirp

Gaussian Pulse Dispersion in Fiber Drag the distance along the fiber and watch a Gaussian pulse spread while its energy stays the same. Find the dispersion length LD, compare two pulse widths, color the pulse by its chirp, and send a stream of bits to see where they merge. Chirp Lab Give the pulse an initial chirp C₀ and see when it first compresses and when it broadens, with the instantaneous frequency across the pulse. Two-Fiber Pulse Lab Send a pulse through standard fiber and then dispersion-compensating fiber. Set the pulse width and each fiber's dispersion and length, and swap the order of the fibers. DCF and DSP Lab Undo the fiber's dispersion in two ways: optically with a dispersion-compensating fiber, or electronically with digital signal processing in a coherent receiver. Link Length Calculator What limits the link, loss or dispersion? Set the bit rate, the source and the fiber and find the longest link you can build. Undo the Fiber A game: a pulse has crossed an unknown length of fiber. Choose the compensating fiber to bring it back to its original width. Signal Bandwidth Lab Pick a sine, square, triangle or Gaussian signal from 1 MHz to 50 GHz and see its FFT spectrum, its bandwidth, what a low-pass filter leaves of it, and how little of the 193 THz optical carrier it occupies.
