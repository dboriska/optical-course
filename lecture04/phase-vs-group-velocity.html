Phase vs Group Velocity

Add two waves of slightly different frequency. The crests of the sum move at the phase velocity v_p = ω/k. The beats, the envelope that carries energy and data, move at the group velocity v_g = Δω/Δk → dω/dk. They differ whenever ω/k changes with k, which is dispersion.

Controls

Set them equal to see a medium without dispersion.
More crests per beat means the two frequencies are closer.

Phase velocitySpeed of one crest of one frequency. A pure sine wave has no start and no end, so it carries no information.
Group velocitySpeed of the envelope made by many frequencies. Energy, pulses and bits travel at v_g.

Speeds are in units of c, and the animation runs slowly: the carrier wavelength is drawn as one grid step. The two waves have k₁,₂ = k₀ ∓ δk and ω₁,₂ = ω(k₁,₂) on a dispersion curve ω(k) that passes through the origin, with ω₀/k₀ = v_p and dω/dk = v_g at k₀. For two waves placed symmetrically on a parabola, Δω/Δk equals the tangent slope exactly. In silica at 1550 nm v_p = c/1.444 and v_g = c/1.463, only 1.3% apart; here the gap is exaggerated so you can see it.
