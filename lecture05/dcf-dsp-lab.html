DCF and DSP Lab

Fiber dispersion only changes the phase of each frequency, H(ω) = exp(jβ₂zω²/2), so it can be undone. A short dispersion-compensating fiber (DCF) with β₂ of the opposite sign does it optically. A coherent receiver measures amplitude and phase and does it in software. A receiver that measures only power cannot. Move along the link, change the DCF, and compare the two receivers.

16 RZ Gaussian bits at 1550 nm, pulse FWHM = 0.4 of the bit slot, propagated exactly with an FFT: each frequency is multiplied by exp(jβ₂zω²/2). SMF: D = 17 ps/(nm·km) (β₂ ≈ −21.7 ps²/km), 0.2 dB/km, effective area 80 µm². DCF: D = −100 ps/(nm·km) (β₂ ≈ +128 ps²/km), about 1 dB/km (slide 27), effective area 20 µm². The order of the fibers does not matter for dispersion (H₁H₂ = H₂H₁), but it matters for the power inside the DCF, where nonlinearity sets in; the warning appears above 10 mW in 20 µm² (0.5 mW/µm²), a rough guide only. Coherent + DSP multiplies the received field by H⁻¹(ω). The power detector has only |A|²; the same filter applied to √|A|² (no phase) cannot undo the spreading. Eye opening: lowest "1" minus highest "0" at the bit centers, relative to the sent pulse peak.
