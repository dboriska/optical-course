Link Length Calculator

Two things stop a fiber link. Loss: the received power falls below the receiver sensitivity, which itself rises with the bit rate. Dispersion: the pulses spread until they spill into the neighboring bit slots. For each bit rate the maximum length is the shorter of the two limits. Change the source, the fiber and the power budget and see which one wins.

Loss limit: L = (Ptx − Psens(B) − margin)/α with Psens(B) = Psens(10 Gb/s) + 10·log10(B/10). Dispersion limit: the criterion 4Bσ ≤ 1 (σ ≤ T/4) with σ² = σ0² + (β2L/2σ0)² + (D L σλ)², where the transmitted Gaussian width σ0 is chosen optimally (σ0² = |β2|L/2). A narrow source gives L = 1/(16B²|β2|); a wide source gives L = 1/(4B|D|σλ). β2 = −λ²D/(2πc) at 1550 nm. The 4Bσ ≤ 1 rule is conservative; the ±1000 ps/nm rule of thumb used in other labs allows about twice the length.
