How a Bragg Grating Picks One Wavelength

A periodic ripple in a waveguide reflects a little light at every tooth (slide 61). Only at the Bragg wavelength λB = 2nΛ do all these small reflections return in phase and build a strong mirror. This is how DFB and DBR lasers select a single mode.

A short grating of 40 periods with coupling κL = 1.44 (peak reflectance 80%), so the band is wide enough to see; real gratings have thousands of periods and a band below 1 nm. Wave amplitudes and the reflection spectrum come from coupled-mode theory: R = κ² sinh²(sL)/(s² cosh²(sL) + Δβ² sinh²(sL)), s² = κ² − Δβ². The phasor diagram is the weak-grating picture: one small reflection per tooth, each delayed by the round trip 2βΛ = 2π λB/λ.
