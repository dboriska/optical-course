Bragg Mirror Lab

A Bragg mirror is many weak reflections. Each period of the index pattern returns a little light, and the returns add in phase only near the Bragg wavelength, λ_B = 2n̄Λ. Build a grating for a DBR laser, or a stack of semiconductor layers for a VCSEL, and see how strong and how narrow the mirror becomes.

Readouts

Exact transfer-matrix calculation at normal incidence. Grating: square index profile n̄ ± Δn/2 with n̄ = 3.2, half a period each, embedded in n̄; coupling coefficient κ = 2Δn/λ_B, peak reflectivity tanh²(κL_g). DBR laser: a 300 µm gain section (n = 3.5, α_int = 10 cm⁻¹) with a cleaved front facet (R = 0.31) and the grating as rear mirror, threshold g_th(λ) = α_int + (1/2L) ln(1/(R₁R(λ))); the gain spectrum is clamped where it first touches the threshold, and the extra phase of the grating is ignored. VCSEL: quarter-wave layers for 980 nm; top mirror from the GaAs cavity to air, bottom mirror AlAs/GaAs from the cavity into the GaAs substrate; active region three 8 nm quantum wells, so the gain needed per round trip is g_th ≈ ln(1/R_topR_bot)/(2 · 24 nm), without internal loss or standing-wave enhancement. The arrow chain is the weak-reflection picture: one arrow per period, each turned by 2π(λ_B/λ − 1) from the one before; it ignores the depletion of the light deep inside a strong mirror.
