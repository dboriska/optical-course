Direct vs Indirect Band Gap

Why GaAs lights up and silicon does not (slide 47). A photon carries almost no momentum, so an electron can only fall vertically in the E(k) diagram. In GaAs the lowest conduction state sits right above the highest valence state; in silicon it does not, and a phonon must supply the missing momentum.

Schematic bands, not to scale. GaAs: direct gap 1.42 eV at k = 0 (Γ). Si: valence-band top at Γ, conduction-band minimum near 0.85·(2π/a) toward X, indirect gap 1.12 eV; the direct gap at Γ is about 3.4 eV. Photon momentum at 870 nm: 2π/λ ≈ 7·10⁶ m⁻¹, against a zone edge 2π/a ≈ 10¹⁰ m⁻¹, about 0.1%. Radiative lifetimes are of order nanoseconds in GaAs and milliseconds in Si.
