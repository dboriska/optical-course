Laser Cavity Lab

A semiconductor chip between two cleaved facets is a Fabry–Perot cavity. Only frequencies that fit a whole number of half-waves are allowed, ν_m = m c/(2nL), and a mode lases when its gain reaches the cavity loss, g_th = α_int + (1/2L) ln(1/R₁R₂). Pump the chip, change the cavity, and replace the facets with a Bragg grating.

Gain model of slide 53: peak gain g_p = σ_g(N − N_T) with σ_g N_T = 100 cm⁻¹. The gain spectrum is a parabola around the peak whose width grows with the pump, and the peak moves to shorter wavelength as the bands fill. The modes and the threshold are exact for the cavity; above threshold the carrier density, and with it the gain, clamps at its threshold value, and each mode gets spontaneous emission amplified ∝ 1/(g_th − g + ε), with ε shrinking as the pump grows. That gives the familiar Fabry–Perot comb under a smooth envelope; the dashed curve is the gain the pump would give without clamping (the picture of slide 59). Modes within 20 dB of the strongest count as lasing. The output spectrum is qualitative. The DFB grating is modeled as low loss in a narrow band around λ_B = 2nΛ and high loss elsewhere. The reach uses B·L·|D|·σ_λ ≤ 1/4 with the rms width of the source, or the transform limit of the 2.5 Gb/s signal when the source is narrower.
