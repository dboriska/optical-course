Semiconductor Gain Lab

Pumping injects electrons into the conduction band and holes into the valence band. Each band fills up to its own quasi-Fermi level (labels give E_fc from E_c and E_fv from E_v), and light is amplified only where stimulated emission beats absorption: E_g < hν < E_fc − E_fv. Raise the carrier density and watch the gain window open.

Parabolic bands (slide 37) with electron and heavy-hole effective masses m_c, m_v; InGaAsP lattice-matched to InP: E_g = 0.80 eV, m_c = 0.045 m₀, m_v = 0.45 m₀; GaAs: E_g = 1.42 eV, m_c = 0.067 m₀, m_v = 0.48 m₀. The quasi-Fermi levels are found from N = ∫ρ_c f_c dE and P = ∫ρ_v (1 − f_v) dE with N = P. A photon hν links E₂ = E_c + (m_r/m_c)(hν − E_g) to E₁ = E₂ − hν at the same k (slide 47). Gain (slides 39–41): g(ν) = (λ²/8πn²τ_r) ρ_cv(ν) [f_c(E₂) − f_v(E₁)], with ρ_cv ∝ √(hν − E_g), n = 3.5 and τ_r = 1 ns. No spectral broadening is included, so the gain starts sharply at E_g; real spectra are smoothed by a few k_BT.
