Mach–Zehnder Modulator Lab

A lithium niobate chip splits the light into two arms, shifts the phase of each with a voltage (the Pockels effect), and recombines them. The phase difference becomes an intensity change, P_out = P_in cos²(Δφ/2) with Δφ = πV/V_π. Bias it, drive it, and see where the light goes when the output is dark.

Ideal modulator of slides 80–84: 50/50 splitter and combiner, no loss, infinite extinction. One arm: φ₁ = πV/V_π, φ₂ = 0. Push-pull: φ₁ = −φ₂ = πV/(2V_π), the same total Δφ with half the voltage on each arm. At the Y-junction the two arms add to the guided output, E_out = (E₁ + E₂)/2, and the out-of-phase part becomes an antisymmetric mode that the single-mode output cannot guide, so it radiates into the substrate. A 2×2 coupler sends that part to its second port, P₂ = P_in sin²(Δφ/2). The output phase (φ₁ + φ₂)/2 is the chirp: zero in push-pull, half the phase swing with one arm. Harmonics are from one period of the sine drive, in dB below the fundamental. The bit pattern is a fixed NRZ sequence with smooth edges.
